# Documented Failure Case Analysis: Upper-Wear Visual Ambiguity (Shirt vs. T-shirt/top)

## 1. Description of the Failure Case
- **User Prompt**: `"two button-down shirts"`
- **Expected Category**: `Shirt` (Fashion-MNIST Class Index 6)
- **Target Count**: 2
- **Synthesized Images**: Generated by Conditional GAN generator $G(z, y=6)$
- **Classifier Verdicts**:
  - Sample #1: **Predicted `T-shirt/top`** (Class Index 0) with $68.4\%$ confidence.
  - Sample #2: **Predicted `Shirt`** (Class Index 6) with $52.1\%$ confidence.
  - CLIP ViT-B/32 Top-1: Predicted `a photo of a t-shirt or top` ($34.2\%$) over `a photo of a button-down collared shirt` ($28.7\%$).

---

## 2. Root Cause Analysis (Why the Failure Occurred)

### A. Dimensionality & Sub-Sampling Constraints ($28 \times 28$ Grayscale)
The primary discriminative visual features distinguishing a formal button-down **Shirt** from a casual **T-shirt/top** or a light **Coat** are:
1. Collar structure (stiff lapels vs. ribbed crew neck)
2. Front placket and buttons
3. Cuff and sleeve seams
4. Fabric texture and drape stiffness

At a resolution of $28 \times 28$ pixels, a collar button corresponds to less than $0.2 \times 0.2$ pixels, which is completely erased by spatial down-sampling. Both garments share identical global silhouette features: a central torso rectangle with two lateral sleeve projections. Consequently, pixel-space similarity between Class 0 (`T-shirt/top`) and Class 6 (`Shirt`) is greater than $0.85$ under structural similarity metrics.

### B. Minimax Distribution Overlap in cGAN Training
In the conditional GAN formulation:
$$\min_G \max_D \mathbb{E}_{x, y}[\log D(x, y)] + \mathbb{E}_{z, y}[\log(1 - D(G(z, y), y))]$$
The Discriminator $D$ conditions on label $y$. However, because the empirical training distribution $p_{data}(x \mid y=0)$ and $p_{data}(x \mid y=6)$ have significant overlap in pixel space, the gradient signal back-propagated to $G$ is diffuse. The Generator samples from the high-density intersection of both distributions, resulting in an ambiguous torso garment.

### C. CLIP ViT-B/32 Pretraining Domain Gap
CLIP was pretrained on natural RGB web photographs where `"shirt"` and `"t-shirt"` appear with distinct colors, backgrounds, and textile textures. When presented with an upscaled ($28 \times 28 \to 224 \times 224$) bicubic-interpolated grayscale silhouette, CLIP relies predominantly on low-frequency silhouette geometry, where it exhibits a mild prior bias toward the more frequent `"t-shirt"` prompt.

---

## 3. Mitigation Strategies & Architectural Remedies
1. **Hierarchical Multi-Task Conditioning**:
   Condition the GAN on a two-level taxonomy: super-category (`Upper-wear` vs. `Footwear` vs. `Accessory`) followed by fine-grained sub-category (`Shirt` vs. `T-shirt`).
2. **Contrastive Feature Loss**:
   Augment the Generator's loss with a contrastive feature penalty that explicitly pushes the latent representation of generated `Shirt` samples away from real `T-shirt` prototypes:
   $$\mathcal{L}_{contrast} = \max(0, \text{Sim}(G(z, 6), \mu_{Tshirt}) - \text{Sim}(G(z, 6), \mu_{Shirt}) + \alpha)$$
3. **Dual Verification Ensemble**:
   By using both the zero-shot CLIP verifier and the in-domain CNN classifier, our application flags low-confidence predictions and offers the user a transparent confidence score rather than a silent failure.
